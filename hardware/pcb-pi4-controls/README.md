# Daily Mirror - Pi 4 case control board (draft v0.4)

> **Status: current, v0.4, not ordered yet.** This is the board for the current box. See [`../README.md`](../README.md) for the current build.

A 2-layer, all-through-hole board that **replaces the printed ButtonCarrier**
in `../enclosure-pi4` and also carries the status LED. Same 1.6 mm thickness,
same two screw holes on the existing button posts, same switch seating plane,
so the cap / switch stack is unchanged. It widens toward the left wall for the
5 mm RGB LED (D1), the Pi header (J1) and three resistors, and takes two more
M2 screws into two new lid posts beside the LED. **Draft for review: not
ordered, not checked against real parts.** Everything is generated by
`build.py` from the case model's numbers.

Renders: `output/board-front-3d.png` (lid side), `output/board-back-3d.png`
(seen from the back, mirrored), `output/board-angle-3d.png`, schematic
`output/schematic.pdf`. In the case: `../enclosure-pi4/output/pcb-*.png`.
Build, cable and test steps: [ASSEMBLY.md](ASSEMBLY.md) (with `board_test.py`).
(`output/` is git-ignored in this repo, as for the other PCB folders.)

## J1 pinout (1x6, 2.54 mm)

Since rev 0.3 the board uses six adjacent pins of the Pi's inner (odd) header column, so one
straight six-wire ribbon with a 1x6 housing at each end connects J1 pin n to
Pi pin 2n-1, with no crossed wires.

| J1 | Net | Pi physical pin | BCM | Goes to |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 3V3 | 1 | 3.3 V | D1 common anode |
| 2 | BTN | 3 | GPIO 2 (1.8 k pull-up on the Pi) | SW1 |
| 3 | LED_B | 5 | GPIO 3 (1.8 k pull-up on the Pi) | R1 330 -> D1 blue |
| 4 | LED_R | 7 | GPIO 4 | R3 330 -> D1 red |
| 5 | GND | 9 | GND | SW1 |
| 6 | LED_G | 11 | GPIO 17 | R2 330 -> D1 green |

GPIO low = colour on. No resistor on the common leg; no pull-up on BTN. I2C
(GPIO 2/3) and 1-Wire (GPIO 4) must stay disabled on this Pi. The service
pins for this board (`device/.env.example` has the preset): button 2, green
17, yellow/blue 3, red 4, `rgb-common-anode`. The table is printed on the
back, between the switch terminals, and "1" / "6" mark the header ends.
(Rev 0.2 used Pi pins 1, 3, 9, 11, 13, 15, which needed crossed jumpers.)

## Layout

Case coordinates (X right, Y up toward the top wall, seen from the front; see
`../enclosure-pi4/README.md`). Board coordinates in KiCad have the origin on
the switch centre: x = X - 18.5, y = 105.75 - Y.

| Item | Case (X, Y) | Side |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Board outline | X 4.0-34.0, Y 93.25-120.35 (30.0 x 27.1 mm, 2 mm corners) | |
| SW1 Omron B3F-4055 | centre (18.5, 105.75), as before | front (lid side) |
| D1 EBOOT 5 mm RGB | centre (30.5, 105.75); leads in a column, R at the bottom (Y 102.75, toward the Pi), then +, G, B upward | front |
| J1 1x6 straight header | X 6.0, pin 1 at Y 96.0 up to pin 6 at Y 108.7 | back, pins toward the tray floor |
| R2 / R3 / R1 (green, red, blue) | rows Y 113.2 / 116.0 / 118.8, pads X 6.0 and 13.62 | back, lying flat |
| Screw holes, 2.4 mm NPTH | (18.5, 96.25), (18.5, 115.25) existing posts; (30.5, 96.25), (30.5, 115.25) new posts | |

- **Switch holes** (rev 0.4) are round 1.1 mm plated holes on the datasheet's
  12.5 x 5.0 mm pitch, inside the same 3.2 x 3.4 mm pads (1.05 mm minimum
  ring). Omron's B3F catalogue recommends 1.2 +-0.05 mm. 1.1 mm is a little
  snugger, so the kinked legs snap in and hold the switch flat before soldering,
  as they do in 0.1" perfboard. The 1.0 x 0.3 mm leg is 1.044 mm across its
  corners, and OSH Park's finished-hole tolerance is +-0.025 mm typical,
  +-0.064 mm worst case. Rev 0.3 had the carrier's 2.0 x 2.2 mm slots
  (`pi4-controls.v0.3-slots.kicad_pcb.bak`). The two boss holes stay 2.0 mm
  (Omron: 1.8 mm for the 1.6 mm bosses). The seating plane is unchanged, since
  the switch body still sits on the board top.
- **SW1 is wired on a diagonal pair** (pad 1 BTN at (24.75, 103.25), pad 2 GND
  at (12.25, 108.25)); the other two terminals are plated but unconnected.
  A diagonal pair is always across the switch, so it does not matter which
  way the B3F pairs its terminals internally.
- **D1 standoff: the flange underside sits 2.9 mm above the board** (7.7 mm
  flange-to-tip LED, board top at Z 26.4, lid face at Z 37). Printed on the
  front silkscreen. Easiest: put the LED through the board unsoldered, screw
  the board onto the lid, push the dome flush with the lid face, solder.
- **Resistors: through-hole axial** (1/4 W, DIN0207, 6.3 x 2.5 mm, 7.62 mm
  pitch). They fit, so no SMD. The rows are 2.8 mm apart; the footprint is the
  stock one with silk/courtyard trimmed to the body (`..._Stacked2.8`).
- **J1: straight, on the back.** The bay under the board is empty down to the
  tray floor (22 mm). A Dupont housing on J1 ends at Z 7.76, 5.3 mm above the
  floor, and the case model reserves 4 mm under it for the wires to turn; the
  housing is 2.55 mm from the Ethernet jack face. A right-angle header was
  rejected: pointing at the Pi it would sit 0.8 mm over the Ethernet jack and
  run into the USB stack; pointing at a wall it needs ~20 mm that is not there.
  Suggested wire route to the GPIO header: down, then along the left wall
  through the 3.3 mm gap between the wall and the Ethernet jack.
- **Routing** (0.5 mm tracks, 0.25 mm clearance): front copper carries 3V3,
  BTN, GND and the green/blue cathodes; back copper carries the three GPIO
  lines from J1 to the resistors and the red cathode. No vias.

## D1: EBOOT 5 mm clear RGB, common anode

From `reference/eboot-rgb-led-5mm-spec.png`: lens 5.0 mm, flange 5.8 x 1.0 mm,
7.7 mm flange to tip, four 0.5 mm leads, common anode the longest. Footprint
`LED_D5.0mm-4_RGB_P2.0mm_Splayed` from the earlier draft: 2.0 mm pitch, 0.9 mm
drills, square anode pad, R / + / G / B on both silk layers. The sheet gives
the lead spacing as 1.0 mm without saying whether that is pitch or gap; 2.0 mm
pads work either way with a slight splay.

## Files

- `build.py` - writes the custom footprints, schematic, board and project, then
  runs ERC, DRC (with schematic parity), netlist, PDF, SVG plots, 3D renders and
  `verify.py`. Reads `../enclosure-pi4/dimensions.json` and
  `../enclosure-pi4/output/layout.json`, so build the case first.
- `verify.py` - netlist and board pads against the J1 table; part sides; part
  and hole positions against the case model; SW1 terminal holes round
  1.1 mm; outline and thickness; ERC/DRC clean. Writes `output/validation.json`.
- `ASSEMBLY.md` - soldering order, the ribbon cable, meter checks, powered test, service settings.
- `board_test.py` - run on the Pi (service stopped): lights R, G, B in turn, prints button events. Untested on hardware.
- `make_models.py` (FreeCADCmd) - coloured VRML models of the LED (at its 2.9 mm
  standoff) and the B3F switch, from the case dimensions.
- `libraries/` - stock KiCad 10.0.6 symbols and footprints (CC-BY-SA-4.0 with
  the libraries exception) plus the custom LED, switch, hole and resistor
  footprints and the two models.

```bash
cd hardware/enclosure-pi4 && ./build.sh                  # case first
FreeCADCmd hardware/pcb-pi4-controls/make_models.py
/usr/bin/python3 hardware/pcb-pi4-controls/build.py --force
```

`--force` overwrites the schematic and board; hand edits in KiCad are lost.

## Assumptions and open questions

- Fab rules are generic (2 layers, 1.6 mm FR4, 0.5 mm tracks, 0.25 mm
  clearance, 0.5 mm copper-to-edge, smallest drill 0.8 mm).
- Screw heads (M2 pan, about 3.8 mm) are kept clear of back copper by the
  routing; the back silk circles show them.
- The 3D models of the LED and switch are drawn from the dimensions, not
  vendor files.
